Setting up Slack for Ship of Tools ​

Ship of Tools can show a Slack channel next to each project, in the Chat drawer (Ctrl+K, Cmd+K on a Mac), and post to it. You connect Slack once, from the drawer, then pick each project's channel there; no file is edited and nothing is restarted. You need to be allowed to add apps to your Slack workspace, or an administrator who approves them.

The chat runs on one computer: the one that runs your project sessions. If you use Ship of Tools on one computer, that is the one, with no hosts.toml needed. If you have several, it is the one your hosts.toml names as hub. Every window shows the chat of that computer, and a project's chat is that of a session row on it.

One Slack workspace, for now. The chat connects one Slack workspace, and every project's channel is a channel of that workspace. An app made in a second workspace is refused at the connect step with "the hub connects one Slack workspace for now, and it is set up for another: the setup guide says how to move it". Channels in several workspaces at once, one workspace per project, are planned and not yet built. Until then, to move the chat to another workspace, remove chat.toml and chat-secrets.toml from the settings folder of the computer the chat runs on (see the end of this page), which clears every project's channel, then connect again.

Once: connect Slack ​

Each person makes their own Slack app, even when a colleague already has one: Slack hands each new message to one of an app's connections, so two people sharing one app's tokens each miss messages.

Open the Chat drawer on a project. While Slack is not connected, the drawer shows set up Slack and leads you through three steps on Slack's website, one screen each; Left and Right move between the steps, PageUp and PageDown (or the mouse wheel) scroll a screen taller than the drawer, which says PageDown for more above its keys, and Escape closes the drawer (opening it again starts over at the first screen). Have Slack's website open and signed in, in your web browser (the Slack desktop app is not enough).

  1. Make the app. Press Enter: your browser opens Slack's page that makes your app, already named Ship of Tools (<the name you sign in to this computer with>) (you can change it there; it is the name Slack shows for your app). The app can list your workspace's channels, read and post in the ones it is in, and, with your own token, post your messages as you; it cannot read direct messages or channels it is not in. Click Next, then Create, then Install to Workspace, then Allow, and press Enter in the drawer. If Slack says the app needs an administrator's approval, send the request and wait for Slack's message that it is approved; then, on the app's page in Slack, click Install to Workspace and Allow. (If you closed the drawer meanwhile, open it and press Right on the first screen to go straight to step 2.) If Slack's page was empty, press Ctrl+C (Cmd+C on a Mac) on this step to copy the app's description, then at api.slack.com/apps click Create New App, From a manifest, choose your workspace, paste it, Next, Create.

  2. The first two tokens. Slack shows the app's User OAuth Token, which starts with xoxp- and lets Ship of Tools post your messages as you, and its Bot User OAuth Token, which starts with xoxb- (if not, at api.slack.com/apps click your app, then OAuth & Permissions). Click Copy beside one, click in the drawer and press Ctrl+V (Cmd+V on a Mac); then do the same with the other, in either order. If the page shows no User OAuth Token, your app was made before Ship of Tools posted as you: on that page, under User Token Scopes, click Add an OAuth Scope and choose chat:write, then again for files:write; then click Reinstall to Workspace and Allow, and the token shows at the top. (These names are Slack's own as its documentation gives them; if its page words them otherwise, follow its words, and the guide will be corrected.)

  3. The last token. Copy it from the same app as the two tokens of step 2: all three tokens come from one app. In Slack's menu on the left, click Basic Information; under App-Level Tokens click Generate Token and Scopes, type a name (messages), click Add Scope, choose connections:write, click Generate, then Copy. Click in the drawer and press Ctrl+V.

A token is a long code, like a password, that lets Ship of Tools act as your app: paste it only in the drawer. The drawer shows a pasted token as its first five characters and its length, and a paste that is not a Slack token is dropped; a paste in these steps never goes into a message. Once all three are pasted, the drawer checks them with Slack (connecting…). Closing the drawer then does not stop the check: the hub finishes it, and a new try waits for its answer. The drawer says connected, with the workspace's ID; the hub keeps the three tokens in its settings folder, readable only by your account, and Enter goes on to choosing the project's channel. If Slack refuses a token, the drawer goes back to its step and says so; the other tokens are kept, so paste the refused one again.

If Slack later stops accepting the saved tokens (the app was removed from the workspace, say), the drawer says so, even while it shows a channel: the hub stops the chat, and the drawer shows its set-up screen in place of the channel. If the App-Level Token the hub holds is another Slack app's (a colleague's, or your own older app's), the hub stops the chat rather than take that app's messages, and the drawer says that the token belongs to another app. The connect cannot tell whose App-Level Token it is: the hub finds out when it first listens, a moment after connected. On either screen, press Enter to open your list of Slack apps: if your app is in it, click it and copy its three tokens again (steps 2 and 3 follow in the drawer); if it is not, press Left to make the app again (step 1). A hub connected before Ship of Tools posted your messages as you, or whose user token Slack stopped accepting, refuses your post with that reason and shows the same screen, titled post as you, in place of the channel: your draft is kept, and the tokens are copied again as above. Paste your own User OAuth Token only: the hub cannot tell whose it is, and would post as that person. To give the hub new tokens at any time, choose Connect Slack again, last in the channel list (Ctrl+Shift+K).

Per project: choose its channel ​

Select the project's row on the computer the chat runs on and open the Chat drawer (Ctrl+K). A project with no channel yet shows the list once the drawer has the keys; press Escape to close it, and Enter later to open it again.

  • Type to narrow the list (case does not matter, and a # is ignored), Up and Down to choose, Enter to pick, Esc to close.

  • A channel the app is not in is marked your app joins it when you pick it: picking a public channel makes the app join it, and Slack then shows that your app joined. An app made by hand without the channels:join permission cannot join, and picking such a channel says so: invite the app to the channel (in it, type /invite @ and your app's name, such as /invite @Ship of Tools (lab-user)).

  • A private channel is in the list only once you have invited your app to it, and is marked private, app invited.

  • If your workspace has more channels than the list holds (1,000), the list says so: paste a channel's link or ID (Ctrl+V) to pick it. A channel's link is in its details in Slack; its last part is the ID, which begins with C or G and is made of capital letters and digits.

  • After you pick, the drawer shows the channel at once. Another project's channel cannot be picked again: it says that another project already uses it.

To change a project's channel, or to give it none, press Ctrl+Shift+K (Cmd+Shift+K on a Mac; chat.setup) in the Chat drawer: the list then begins with No channel for this project, and ends with Connect Slack again. The window writes the choice into chat.toml for you, whole: comments and the order of its keys are not kept. One channel per project.

Setting it up by hand ​

The drawer writes the same two files a person can write. To write them yourself instead:

  1. At api.slack.com/apps, choose Create New App, then From a manifest, pick your workspace, paste this (with your own name in the two names; Slack takes at most 35 characters), then Create, Install to Workspace, Allow:

    yaml
    display_information:
  name: Ship of Tools (lab-user)
features:
  bot_user:
    display_name: Ship of Tools (lab-user)
    always_online: true
oauth_config:
  scopes:
    user:
      - chat:write
      - files:write
    bot:
      - channels:history
      - channels:read
      - channels:join
      - groups:history
      - groups:read
      - users:read
      - chat:write
      - chat:write.customize
      - files:read
      - files:write
settings:
  event_subscriptions:
    bot_events:
      - message.channels
      - message.groups
  org_deploy_enabled: false
  socket_mode_enabled: true
  token_rotation_enabled: false

    Copy the User OAuth Token (xoxp-) and the Bot User OAuth Token (xoxb-), and make an App-Level Token with the scope connections:write as in step 3 above (xapp-).

  2. Open Slack in a web browser and click the channel. The address looks like https://app.slack.com/client/T0123ABCD/C0456EFGH: T0123ABCD is your workspace's ID and C0456EFGH the channel's. Invite your app to the channel.

  3. In the hub's settings folder (~/.config/sot on Linux and macOS, %LOCALAPPDATA%\sot\config on Windows), make chat-secrets.toml, readable only by you (on Linux or macOS, chmod 600 ~/.config/sot/chat-secrets.toml):

    toml
    workspace = "T0123ABCD"
bot_token = "xoxb-..."
user_token = "xoxp-..."
app_token = "xapp-..."

    and chat.toml, which says which channel goes with which project folder (the folder as its session row shows it; on Windows in single quotes, folder = 'C:\Users\you\projects\my-project'):

    toml
    workspace = "T0123ABCD"
channels = ["C0456EFGH"]

[[mapping]]
folder = "/home/you/projects/my-project"
channel = "C0456EFGH"

  4. Restart Ship of Tools' background service on the hub (on Linux: systemctl --user restart sotd). Your sessions keep running; their Julia REPLs start fresh. A file edited by hand is read at the service's start; a change from the window applies at once.

If it does not show ​

The Chat drawer's title says what is wrong. no channel means no channel is set for this project: pick one as above, or, if you wrote chat.toml by hand, the folder in it is not exactly the row's folder. the hub has no usable chat configuration means a file was refused: the background service's log says which file and which line (sotd log-path prints where the log is). A token or ID is never printed in the log. The full rules for both files are in the configuration reference.
