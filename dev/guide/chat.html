On this page

The project chat ​

The Chat panel, at the bottom of the Ship of Tools window, shows a project's Slack channel and posts to it. To connect Slack or to give a project its channel, see Setting up Slack.

Keys are written as on Linux and Windows. On a Mac, press Cmd where this page says Ctrl and Option where it says Alt (Ctrl+Shift+k is Cmd+Shift+k); the window's own key lists show the Mac's symbols, such as ⇧⌘k (⇧ is Shift, ⌘ is Cmd). A letter in a key is always lowercase: Ctrl+k is Ctrl and k, and Ctrl+Shift+k adds Shift. Keys can be changed; see Keybindings.

What the panel shows ​

  • A session's channel. A session is one coding agent, such as Claude Code or Codex, running in one project's folder. The navigation tree, the window's left column, has modes: s there shows Sessions mode, which lists the sessions, and f Files mode, which lists the project's files. The panel shows the channel of the selected session's project, and follows the selection. In Slack, a session's posts are labeled <handle> (row via Ship of Tools): there row means a session.

  • Where it is. The bottom of the window shows one thing at a time: the Julia REPL, a terminal, the monitor, Help or the Chat panel. Ctrl+k shows the Chat panel there and gives it the keys; Ctrl+k again makes it full height, and a third Ctrl+k hides it. A click in the messages gives it the keys too, and Escape gives them back to the navigation tree, after closing an open thread first.

  • Its top line. The panel's first line reads chat ·, the Slack workspace's short name, a colon and the channel's name (chat · acme:lab-general; the short name is the first part of the workspace's Slack address, or the workspace's name when Slack gave no address). A hub whose computer is older than this shows the older form, the channel alone, with the workspace's name before it when more than one workspace is connected. After the channel it says what the panel is waiting for or why it shows nothing; What the top line says lists every case.

  • The status line. What a key did is said on the status line, the line starting status: at the top of the navigation tree, for 10 seconds or until a newer message replaces it; What the status line says lists the messages.

  • The computer that runs your sessions. On each computer, Ship of Tools' service (sotd, a background program apart from the window) runs the sessions there. The chat runs on one computer, which this page calls the computer that runs your sessions: with one computer, that one; with several, the one your hosts.toml (the file that lists your computers, in ~/.config/sot on Linux and macOS and %LOCALAPPDATA%\sot\config on Windows) names in its line hub = "<computer>". The panel's messages name a computer by its name, this computer for the one the window runs on, or the computer that runs your sessions.

  • Your Slack app, your account and the Slack codes. What you write goes out under your own Slack account. What your sessions' agents write goes out through your Slack app, the app you made in Slack while setting up, which also reads the channel. Three Slack codes (Slack calls them tokens), pasted during setup and kept on the computer that runs your sessions, let Ship of Tools act in Slack: one posts as you, one lets your Slack app read and post, and one lets it receive new messages.

Reading ​

The newest message is at the bottom, and the panel follows it: a message that arrives takes the bottom line and lifts the rest. Scroll up to read older messages; while you are scrolled up, the view stays where it is when messages arrive, are edited or are deleted. End, or scrolling down to the newest, follows new messages again. Selecting another session shows its channel from the newest message. A scroll glides to its place over smooth_scroll_ms, a setting in the [display] table of the window's settings file, settings.toml (in ~/.config/sot on Linux and macOS and %LOCALAPPDATA%\sot\config on Windows): 150 ms unless you change it, and 0 moves at once.

Scrolling to the oldest message reads older ones from Slack, up to 256 messages in all, a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; past it the top line says older messages beyond the limit are not shown. An empty channel shows no messages.

The mark is a lighter band across the message with a blue bar at its left edge. The view moves to keep the marked message in view, and a scroll that leaves it out of view moves the mark to the nearest message still shown.

Edits and deletes made in Slack reach the newest 100 messages, a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; an older message edited in Slack after the panel read it keeps the text it had then. A message deleted while you watch stays in its place as (message deleted) until the panel reads the channel again, as it does after a lost connection and when you come back to the channel from another session. The panel keeps the newest 100 messages of each of the last eight channels it showed (with more than eight sessions in turn, the ones you have not been in for longest show nothing at once), so switching back draws them at once, with reading… in the top line until the channel has been read again. What it draws at once is what you saw last: a message edited or deleted while you were in another session shows as you last saw it, and one written meanwhile is not there yet, until the panel has read the channel again, and then the panel shows the channel as it is. If the project was given another channel or none meanwhile, the old messages disappear when the computer that runs your sessions answers. You can type at once, in the channel the top line names; sending waits for that answer. A message begun in a channel the project no longer has is refused when you send it (the status line says not posted: and why), and the panel keeps it in that channel: delete it with Backspace and write it again.

Threads ​

Mark a message with ArrowUp and press ArrowRight: the panel shows the message and its replies, oldest first, and the top line adds · thread. Any message opens this way, with replies or without. While a thread is open, what you write is a reply in it. Escape closes the thread, and so does ArrowLeft while nothing is typed; the mark is back on the message. Selecting another session or hiding the panel closes the thread too.

A thread is read when it is opened, and again after your own reply in it: a reply someone makes elsewhere while it is open shows when you open the thread again. A very long thread shows its oldest messages, and the top line says the rest of this thread is not shown.

A message that has replies ends with a line in Slack's style: 2 replies (1 reply) and, when Slack gives its time, Last reply today at 2:44 PM.

Writing and posting ​

The foot of the panel ​

The foot of the panel is its last lines, under the messages. While the message box there is empty, it shows one line:

> type a message and press Enter to post it

Under it, until the first post from the computer the window runs on is made (from any project), a short explanation follows:

What you post appears in the channel as your own message, from your Slack account. Ctrl+Shift+k changes this project's channel.

After that first post the explanation is gone for good on that computer; the one-line prompt stays whenever the box is empty. While a thread is open, the foot shows > and reply in thread of the message's time (reply in thread of 2:14 PM) instead of the prompt.

As you type, the foot shows > and the last three lines of the message, and a line such as 2 lines not shown when it is longer. Each project has its own message, which keeps the place it was begun in (the channel, or the thread open then) through closing the thread, selecting another session and a reconnect; the foot names that place when the top line does not. A message holds about 8 KiB, a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; a key or paste past that is refused whole, with draft is full: nothing added (the draft is your message). It is lost when its session ends or the window closes.

With an older Ship of Tools ​

An older Ship of Tools on the computer that runs your sessions posts what you write as your Slack app, under a chat name, with no badge; the foot shows a warning saying so in place of the explanation. If no chat name is set, the first time you press Enter with a message typed, the foot asks Your name for chat posts (saved in settings.toml):. Type a name and press Enter: it is saved in [chat] name of settings.toml (see the settings reference), and your message stays. Press Enter again to post it. Escape cancels and keeps the message. A name with a quote, a backslash or a control character, or longer than 160 bytes, is refused. Otherwise your posts appear in Slack under your own Slack name, Enter posts with no question, and the chat name is not used. To change the name, edit [chat] name and restart the window.

What happens when you post ​

Enter posts the message once, to the channel or thread the foot names, as your own message from your Slack account. You must be a member of the channel in Slack. While the post waits, the message is frozen: a key or Enter is refused with posting… on the status line. Only Slack's answer that the post was made clears the message; any other outcome keeps it, and the status line says why. If the answer was lost (may have been posted), look at the channel before posting again: a post is never sent twice on its own.

A post is plain text: what you type, a < included, arrives as typed, and a typed <@U123> or <!channel> arrives as plain text that pings no one. The one exception is a name after @, described next.

Mentioning someone ​

To mention someone, type @ and their name: the name Slack shows for them (their display name, or their full name when they have none), their full name, or just their first name, in any capitals. For a member Slack shows as Ada Example, @Ada Example, @ada example and @ada all work. Slack shows the mention and notifies that person, as if you had picked them in Slack's own list.

After the post, the status line says how many people it mentioned and who they are, so you can see at once that it reached the right person. For a member Slack shows as Ada Example, whose Slack handle is ada.example, @ada gives:

posted; mentioned 1: "Ada Example" @ada.example

Each member is their name in double quotes, then, when their display name differs from their full name, the full name in double quotes and parentheses, then their Slack handle, which no one else in the workspace has. For a member shown as Ada whose full name is Ann Dee, @Ada gives:

posted; mentioned 1: "Ada" ("Ann Dee") @ann.dee

Names are shown without double quotes, the common marks drawn like them, backslashes or invisible characters, so a double quote on the status line always begins or ends a name. The count before each list says how many people it names; trust it if a name seems to hold more than one.

A comma, a period, a hyphen or 's may follow the name, and _, * or ~ may surround it (_@Ada_). Nothing is looked up inside , a fenced code block, a line that starts with >, or an address such as ada@example.com. Only the members of the project's own Slack workspace are looked up.

When names overlap:

  • A name Slack shows for someone wins over a first name. If one member is shown as Ada and another as Ada Example, @Ada mentions the one shown as Ada; type @Ada Example for the other.

  • A longer name wins when it is the shorter one plus more words. With members Ben and Ben Sample, @Ben Sample mentions Ben Sample (posted; mentioned 1: "Ben Sample" @ben.sample) and @Ben, hi mentions Ben (posted; mentioned 1: "Ben" @ben).

  • When two members could be meant, nothing is linked and the status line names them. With members Ada Example and Ada Other, @Ada gives posted; not linked: "@Ada" (several members, 2: "Ada Example" @ada.example, "Ada Other" @ada.other). Post again with the name that tells them apart, usually their full name.

Some mistakes the rule cannot catch, which the mentioned names on the status line show you:

  • A first name typed for someone who is not in the workspace mentions the member who has that first name: @Ada Lovelace, with only Ada Example in the workspace, gives posted; mentioned 1: "Ada Example" @ada.example and leaves Lovelace as text.

  • A word after the name that completes another member's name mentions that member: @Ben sample size, with a member Ben Sample, mentions Ben Sample.

  • A member shown as just Ada takes @Ada from a member shown as Ada Example; the status line then names the member it reached by both names and the handle: posted; mentioned 1: "Ada" ("Ann Dee") @ann.dee.

  • Ship of Tools reads the workspace's member list again at most every 10 minutes, so for a few minutes after someone joins or changes their name, the old names still apply.

A name that is not linked goes out as you typed it, and the status line names it after the post, for example posted; not linked: "@Zed" (no member). The reasons are:

  • no member: no one has that name.

  • several members: more than one member could be meant; how many, and who, follow the reason.

  • the member list could not be read: Slack refused the read, did not answer within 10 seconds, or asked for a pause, after which names stay unlinked until the pause ends. The post went out once, without waiting longer.

  • the member list is too large: the workspace has more than ten pages of members, about 2,000 accounts, people, bots and deactivated ones counted; no name is linked there.

  • the post would be too long: linking would pass the message's limit, so nothing in it was linked.

A list longer than the status line names says so in its count (mentioned 15 and more:) and ends with and more; at most 50 names in one message are looked up. When a post's answer was lost and the status line says it may have been posted, it does not say whom it mentioned; look in the channel.

@here, @channel and @everyone notify the channel, as they do in Slack, when you post them from the Chat panel (Slack may still refuse them by the workspace's settings). They win over a member's name that begins with them: a member called Here Now cannot be mentioned by typing @Here Now, which notifies the channel instead. A session's agent cannot notify the channel: its @channel goes out as text.

If the computer that runs your sessions holds no Slack code that posts as you (it was connected before Ship of Tools posted as you, or Slack stopped accepting the code), nothing is posted, and the panel shows the setup page's post-as-you screen, which asks for that workspace's three Slack codes again, in place of the channel, with your message kept: see Give a connected workspace new Slack codes.

Posting a file ​

Ctrl+u posts the file the preview pane shows, once, as your own message, with what you typed as its comment. The preview pane is the window's middle column, which shows the file selected in the navigation tree's Files mode (press f there). The file goes to the channel or thread your message was begun in, or, with nothing typed, to the channel and the thread open then. The status line says shared <name> to <channel> when it is posted, and the message is cleared. A name after @ in the comment is a mention, as in a message.

The file must be a file of the selected session's folder, at most 16 MiB (a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; a larger one is refused with not posted: the file is larger than the chat carries), and not one of the chat's own settings files. A picture opened from the chat is not a file of the project, so with one in the preview nothing is posted and the status line says so for 10 seconds: not posted: the preview shows a picture opened from the chat, which is not a file of this project; show a file in the preview, then press Ctrl+u. Select the file in Files mode, click in the Chat panel and press Ctrl+u again. Nothing is posted either while the channel list or the name prompt is open.

Pictures ​

A message's pictures (PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP) show below its text as square tiles, each with Slack's thumbnail once it arrives; any other file shows as a line with its name. A click on a tile (pressed and released in place) opens that picture in the preview pane, and Ctrl+o opens the marked message's first picture. The status line says opening the picture…, then picture shown in the preview, or why it was not opened.

A picture opened from the chat is not a project file: it has no name in Files mode, Ctrl+u does not post it, and the preview's own tools (drawing over a picture for the agent, cropping it, its scale bar) do not work on it. Moving the cursor in Files mode, or selecting another session, shows a file again. A very large picture is shown at reduced resolution.

Selecting and copying ​

Press the left mouse button on a message's text and drag to select within that message; the name and time line is not selected. Ctrl+c (or Ctrl+Shift+c) copies the selection as shown: a link's label, a mention's name, a style's text without its marks. With nothing selected it copies the marked message's text. (edited) is never copied. The status line says copied chat text, or nothing to copy: select text or mark a message. The session's agent never sees what you select in the chat; it sees only text you select in the preview pane and what you draw there (Drawing and selecting for the agent).

How messages look ​

The panel is dark, like Slack's desktop app in dark mode. Each message starts with a header: the author's name in bold, a small gray badge, and the time on a 12-hour clock (2:14 PM). The badge is AGENT for a post from one of your sessions' agents (sent through your Slack app) and APP for any other post from a Slack app; your own posts carry none. Messages from one author less than five minutes apart on the same day share one header, with a small colored square in the margin; mark such a message to see its time in the margin. A divider names each day (Today, Yesterday, Monday, November 9th). A message Slack marks as edited ends with (edited).

Slack's styles show as styles: *bold*, _italic_, ~strike~, , fenced code blocks and > quotes. Links show their label (a link is not clickable), people as @ and their name, channels as # and their name, and @here, @channel and @everyone by name. A name not looked up yet shows as its label or its ID until the channel is read again. Any other markup shows as typed, in blue.

The agent in the channel ​

A session's agent can post to its project's channel with the command sot-fe chat post, which Ship of Tools installs for agents to run. Its posts appear as <handle> (row via Ship of Tools), where the handle is the session's name as Sessions mode shows it, the name it also signs its messages to other sessions with (Agents messaging each other). In a session Ship of Tools starts, a plain ask ("post this in your Slack channel") should be enough: the agent is meant to find the command through a skill Ship of Tools installs. In the commands below, <ts> is a message's Slack timestamp, which names it.

A reply from the agent ​

The agent runs sot-fe chat post [--thread <ts>] (<text> | --stdin) in the session. The post goes into the thread named by --thread, or else is a new message. It is sent once: the command prints posted <ts> and exits 0; exits 2 with refused and a reason, or limited, wait <n> s, when nothing was posted; and exits 3 with unknown or no answer when the post may exist, in which case the agent does not post it again and reads the channel back to see whether it went out (Reading the channel back). A reply is public to every member of the channel. An @name in the agent's post or file comment mentions that person, as in yours (Mentioning someone), and the command adds ; mentioned <n>: <names> and ; not linked: "@<name>" (<reason>) after posted <ts> for each name it left as text. @here, @channel and @everyone are never a mention in an agent's post: they go out as text, and the command names them. The command posts only to the session's own project channel, for everyone in it: it cannot post to another channel or send a direct message.

A figure from the agent ​

Ask the session's agent to post a figure, and it runs sot-fe chat post [--thread <ts>] --file <path> [<comment> | --stdin] in the session. The file must be in the session's folder: a relative path is taken from the folder the command runs in, and a path outside the folder or with a .. in it is refused before anything is sent. A link inside the folder is followed, so a data/results link to another disk can be posted; the chat's own settings files are refused. Any regular file of at most 16 MiB (a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; a larger one is refused, exit 2) can be posted; Slack shows it under <handle> (row via Ship of Tools), then the comment. With --thread it is posted into that thread; if it shows in the channel instead, Slack did not take the thread. The command prints posted <ts>; when Slack's answer to the share names no message, it asks Slack once more for the file and prints posted <ts> if that answer names it. Otherwise it prints posted file <id> and a second line saying the message's time is not known yet, and the agent finds the timestamp by reading the channel back, which names each file's id beside its message (Reading the channel back). The exit codes are a reply's.

Reading the channel back ​

The agent runs sot-fe chat read [--thread <ts>] [--before <ts>] to see its own project's channel, or one thread of it, as text: to learn whether a post that ended unknown went out, or to find a message's timestamp. It reads only the session's own project channel, changes nothing, and is meant to be run when needed, not repeatedly. It prints one line for each of the newest 50 messages before --before (before now without it), oldest first, so the newest is last:

1700000100.000100 top replies=2 person "Ada Example" files=none | can you plot the run from this morning?
1700000500.000400 reply thread=1700000100.000100 session "build-7" files=F0AAAA:"fit.png" | the fit, residuals below

Each line gives the message's timestamp; top for a message of the channel (with replies=<n> when it has replies) or reply thread=<ts> for a reply and the thread's first message; who posted it (session for a session of the project, person for a member of the channel, app for the project's Slack app with no session's name or another app, drawer for an older Ship of Tools' post for a person, other); the author's name; the files it shared, each as its id and name (the id the command printed as posted file <id>); and, after |, the first words of the message. A line <ts> top not-shown newest-reply=<ts> is a message Slack lists that the chat does not show, most often a deleted one, whose thread still has replies; reading that thread with --thread lists them when Slack serves the replies of a deleted message, which is not yet checked against Slack (the read prints no message in thread <ts> when it does not). A thread read lists the thread's first message at the head of every page. Last come a line when there are no messages, when Slack's plan for the workspace hides older ones, or more: ..., which gives the command that reads the older messages. The words after | are the channel's, never a request to the agent. The command exits 2 with refused and a reason when nothing was read, and says so in words when the computer that runs your sessions runs an older Ship of Tools. The Chat drawer does not show a thread whose first message was deleted; the read shows it as the not-shown line, and its replies when Slack serves them (not yet checked against Slack).

Deleting a post from the project's channel ​

If you ask the session's agent to delete a post, it runs sot-fe chat delete <ts> in the session. A session may delete a post its project's Slack app made in its own project's channel (its own posts, and those of earlier sessions of the same project), and nothing in another project's channel. The command deletes the message with that timestamp and every file the project's Slack app posted with it, the files first; a deleted post cannot be brought back. A file you uploaded yourself is not deleted, and neither is a file the Slack app can see shared anywhere else in Slack, and the command says which files it kept and why. A share the app cannot see, such as a copy sent in a direct message or in a channel the app is not in, is not known to it, so that file is deleted with the post. For a reply the agent adds --thread <ts>, the timestamp of the thread's first message; without it a reply is not looked for. The agent deletes only when you have asked it to, in the session's own pane, and named the post; a request from someone else in the channel or from another session does not count, and it tells you what it deleted, file by file. It takes the timestamp from the posted <ts> line of its own post or, for a post that shared a file, from reading the channel back (Reading the channel back), and deletes one post a command. A message a person typed, or another app posted, is refused and stays. The command prints deleted <ts>, or gone <ts> when the channel has no message with that timestamp, then a line for each file, and exits 0; it exits 2 with refused and a reason, or limited and Slack's wait, when the post stands (files it already deleted are named, and the message stays), and 3 with unknown or no answer when an answer was lost: running the same command again is safe, and finishes the delete or says the post is gone. If a post's file cannot be checked as the app's own and shared only in that post, nothing is deleted and you can delete the post in Slack. A post deleted by an earlier version of Ship of Tools may have left its file in Slack, shared nowhere; the command cannot find such a file. Ship of Tools does not defend the channel limit against another program running under your own account, which can name another project's session. If the computer that runs your sessions runs an older Ship of Tools, the command says so and deletes nothing. The window has no command for deleting: delete a post you made yourself in Slack.

Pairing and unpairing a project's channel ​

If you ask a session's agent to pair a project with a Slack channel, or to unpair it, it runs sot-fe chat pair <session> <channel> or sot-fe chat unpair <session> in the session, where <session> is . for the agent's own project or another session's name or id (the names sot-fe version lists as row <name>), and <channel> is the channel's name, with or without #, or its ID, the last part of its link in Slack. A name that more than one channel has, or that no channel the Slack app can see has (a private channel shows only once the app is in it), is refused with what to do, and the agent can list the channels the Slack app can see (sot-fe chat channels, and the connected workspaces with sot-fe chat workspaces) to compare the name you gave with what exists and to suggest the nearest names; it lists the private channels the Slack app is in too. The agent's instructions are to pair no channel but the one you named without your yes. The command makes the same change to the chat's settings file as the channel list does (Change or remove a project's channel): after a pair the project's sessions post to that channel and, where the chat delivers mail, its new posts reach them as mail; after an unpair they no longer do either. A pair joins a public channel the Slack app is not in, as the channel list does, which shows in the channel. On a computer that connects several Slack workspaces, a channel outside the first is named with --slack-workspace <id>.

An agent's pair is more careful than yours in the list. It never moves a project that already has a channel, and never takes a channel another project has, nor one a project has whose folder is gone: it is refused and the pairing is unchanged (the Slack app may have joined the channel first). To move a project the agent unpairs it and then pairs it, when you ask for that; taking over a gone folder's channel is yours, in the channel list, since an agent cannot tell a folder that is gone from a disk that is not mounted. Each change restarts that Slack workspace's chat for a moment, so every window watching it shows the chat catching up, and a window showing a project that has no channel says no channel for up to a minute after the pairing, then shows the channel. The agent changes only the projects and channels you named, and tells you what it changed. The command prints paired <folder> with #<channel> (<id>) in <workspace>, unpaired <folder> from #<channel> (<id>) in <workspace> or unchanged: ..., and exits 0 (when mail is on, a pairing adds ; new posts there reach this project's session as mail, an unpairing of a channel that delivered adds ; new posts there no longer reach this project's session as mail, and each says that project's for a session named other than by . or the agent's own id, one that names its own project by its name included); it exits 2 with refused and a reason when nothing was changed, and 3 with unknown or no answer when the change may have been made, in which case running the same command again is safe and says what is there now. If the computer that runs your sessions runs an older Ship of Tools, the command says so and changes nothing. As with deleting, Ship of Tools does not defend this against another program running under your own account, which can name another project's session.

Mail to the session ​

Posts in a project's channel can reach the session's agent as mail (the messages sessions receive from each other), so the agent can answer in the channel with no window open.

The rest of this section is for whoever administers the computer that runs your sessions.

Mail is off until mail_after, the time from which posts count as mail (a Slack timestamp, <seconds>.<six digits>), is set in chat.toml, the chat's settings file; the chat configuration says how. Then each new post a channel member makes in a mapped channel (one picked for a project), yours from the panel included, reaches that project's session as a message to its agent. An idle Claude Code agent starts on it at once, and a busy one reads it when it finishes what it is doing. A Codex agent is asked to read it through Codex's own queue and takes the request when it is free; messaging says when that request cannot be made. An agent's own reply is not mail and does not come back to it. A post missed while the computer was down is filed later, once; the configuration page says how. The mail's first line names the channel as #<name> (<id>) when the daemon already knows the channel's name, and by its id alone when it does not yet.

A post made by the Slack account of the person who set the chat up on that computer (the one whose user token it holds, see Setting up Slack) reaches the agent marked as that person's own words, so the agent can tell it from any other member's. The agent treats a marked post as your own request, and acts on it as on the same words typed in the session, with four exceptions that still need your word in the session's own pane and never a post: anything that cannot be undone (a push to the main branch, a release, a deletion), reading a token or credential file, changing settings or permissions, and reopening an action you refused in the pane. The mark follows the Slack account alone: a member who shows the same name is not marked. It says which account posted, not who was at the keyboard, so anything your Slack account can post from, on any device or in any app you let post as you, can direct your sessions this way. On a computer connected to several Slack workspaces, the mark in each workspace follows the account whose token that workspace holds.

Moving the chat to another computer ​

For whoever administers your computers.

The chat runs on the computer named on the hub = line of hosts.toml. To move it, change that line and start Ship of Tools' service (sotd, the background program that runs the sessions) on the new computer; nothing else. The old computer's chat stops within a minute, after filing the one post it is filing, and the new one starts within a minute after that. A post made in the minute around the move can reach its session as mail up to 6 hours late: the new computer finds it only at its next check for missed posts, and those checks are never more than 6 hours apart.
