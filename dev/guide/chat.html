The project chat ​

The Chat panel, at the bottom of the Ship of Tools window, shows a project's Slack channel and posts to it. To connect Slack or to give a project its channel, see Setting up Slack.

Keys are written as on Linux and Windows. On a Mac, press Cmd where this page says Ctrl and Option where it says Alt (Ctrl+Shift+k is Cmd+Shift+k); the window's own key lists show the Mac's symbols, such as ⇧⌘k (⇧ is Shift, ⌘ is Cmd). A letter in a key is always lowercase: Ctrl+k is Ctrl and k, and Ctrl+Shift+k adds Shift. Keys can be changed; see Keybindings.

What the panel shows ​

  • A session's channel. A session is one coding agent, such as Claude Code or Codex, running in one project's folder. The navigation tree, the window's left column, has modes: s there shows Sessions mode, which lists the sessions, and f Files mode, which lists the project's files. The panel shows the channel of the selected session's project, and follows the selection. In Slack, a session's posts are labeled <handle> (row via Ship of Tools): there row means a session.

  • Where it is. The bottom of the window shows one thing at a time: the Julia REPL, a terminal, the monitor, Help or the Chat panel. Ctrl+k shows the Chat panel there and gives it the keys; Ctrl+k again makes it full height, and a third Ctrl+k hides it. A click in the messages gives it the keys too, and Escape gives them back to the navigation tree, after closing an open thread first.

  • Its top line. The panel's first line reads chat · and the channel (chat · #lab-general); when more than one Slack workspace is connected it names the workspace first (chat · Alpha Lab · #lab-general). After the channel it says what the panel is waiting for or why it shows nothing; What the top line says lists every case.

  • The status line. What a key did is said on the status line, the line starting status: at the top of the navigation tree, for 10 seconds or until a newer message replaces it; What the status line says lists the messages.

  • The computer that runs your sessions. On each computer, Ship of Tools' service (sotd, a background program apart from the window) runs the sessions there. The chat runs on one computer, which this page calls the computer that runs your sessions: with one computer, that one; with several, the one your hosts.toml (the file that lists your computers, in ~/.config/sot on Linux and macOS and %LOCALAPPDATA%\sot\config on Windows) names in its line hub = "<computer>". The panel's messages name a computer by its name, this computer for the one the window runs on, or the computer that runs your sessions.

  • Your Slack app, your account and the Slack codes. What you write goes out under your own Slack account. What your sessions' agents write goes out through your Slack app, the app you made in Slack while setting up, which also reads the channel. Three Slack codes (Slack calls them tokens), pasted during setup and kept on the computer that runs your sessions, let Ship of Tools act in Slack: one posts as you, one lets your Slack app read and post, and one lets it receive new messages.

Reading ​

The newest message is at the bottom, and the panel follows it: a message that arrives takes the bottom line and lifts the rest. Scroll up to read older messages; while you are scrolled up, the view stays where it is when messages arrive, are edited or are deleted. End, or scrolling down to the newest, follows new messages again. Selecting another session shows its channel from the newest message. A scroll glides to its place over smooth_scroll_ms, a setting in the [display] table of the window's settings file, settings.toml (in ~/.config/sot on Linux and macOS and %LOCALAPPDATA%\sot\config on Windows): 150 ms unless you change it, and 0 moves at once.

Scrolling to the oldest message reads older ones from Slack, up to 256 messages in all, a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; past it the top line says older messages beyond the limit are not shown. An empty channel shows no messages.

The mark is a lighter band across the message with a blue bar at its left edge. The view moves to keep the marked message in view, and a scroll that leaves it out of view moves the mark to the nearest message still shown.

Edits and deletes made in Slack reach the newest 100 messages, a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; an older message edited in Slack after the panel read it keeps the text it had then. A message deleted while you watch stays in its place as (message deleted) until the panel reads the channel again, as it does after a lost connection and when you come back to the channel from another session. The panel keeps the messages of the last two channels it showed, so switching back draws them as soon as the computer that runs your sessions answers, with reading… in the top line until the channel is read again.

Threads ​

Mark a message with ArrowUp and press ArrowRight: the panel shows the message and its replies, oldest first, and the top line adds · thread. Any message opens this way, with replies or without. While a thread is open, what you write is a reply in it. Escape closes the thread, and so does ArrowLeft while nothing is typed; the mark is back on the message. Selecting another session or hiding the panel closes the thread too.

A thread is read when it is opened, and again after your own reply in it: a reply someone makes elsewhere while it is open shows when you open the thread again. A very long thread shows its oldest messages, and the top line says the rest of this thread is not shown.

A message that has replies ends with a line in Slack's style: 2 replies (1 reply) and, when Slack gives its time, Last reply today at 2:44 PM.

Writing and posting ​

The foot of the panel ​

The foot of the panel is its last lines, under the messages. While the message box there is empty, it shows one line:

> type a message and press Enter to post it

Under it, until the first post from the computer the window runs on is made (from any project), a short explanation follows:

What you post appears in the channel as your own message, from your Slack account. Ctrl+Shift+k changes this project's channel.

After that first post the explanation is gone for good on that computer; the one-line prompt stays whenever the box is empty. While a thread is open, the foot shows > and reply in thread of the message's time (reply in thread of 2:14 PM) instead of the prompt.

As you type, the foot shows > and the last three lines of the message, and a line such as 2 lines not shown when it is longer. Each project has its own message, which keeps the place it was begun in (the channel, or the thread open then) through closing the thread, selecting another session and a reconnect; the foot names that place when the top line does not. A message holds about 8 KiB, a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; a key or paste past that is refused whole, with draft is full: nothing added (the draft is your message). It is lost when its session ends or the window closes.

With an older Ship of Tools ​

An older Ship of Tools on the computer that runs your sessions posts what you write as your Slack app, under a chat name, with no badge; the foot shows a warning saying so in place of the explanation. If no chat name is set, the first time you press Enter with a message typed, the foot asks Your name for chat posts (saved in settings.toml):. Type a name and press Enter: it is saved in [chat] name of settings.toml (see the settings reference), and your message stays. Press Enter again to post it. Escape cancels and keeps the message. A name with a quote, a backslash or a control character, or longer than 160 bytes, is refused. Otherwise your posts appear in Slack under your own Slack name, Enter posts with no question, and the chat name is not used. To change the name, edit [chat] name and restart the window.

What happens when you post ​

Enter posts the message once, to the channel or thread the foot names, as your own message from your Slack account. You must be a member of the channel in Slack. While the post waits, the message is frozen: a key or Enter is refused with posting… on the status line. Only Slack's answer that the post was made clears the message; any other outcome keeps it, and the status line says why. If the answer was lost (may have been posted), look at the channel before posting again: a post is never sent twice on its own.

A post is plain text: what you type, a < included, arrives as typed, and @here and <!channel> arrive as plain text that pings no one.

If the computer that runs your sessions holds no Slack code that posts as you (it was connected before Ship of Tools posted as you, or Slack stopped accepting the code), nothing is posted, and the panel shows the setup page's post-as-you screen, which asks for that workspace's three Slack codes again, in place of the channel, with your message kept: see Give a connected workspace new Slack codes.

Posting a file ​

Ctrl+u posts the file the preview pane shows, once, as your own message, with what you typed as its comment. The preview pane is the window's middle column, which shows the file selected in the navigation tree's Files mode (press f there). The file goes to the channel or thread your message was begun in, or, with nothing typed, to the channel and the thread open then. The status line says shared <name> to <channel> when it is posted, and the message is cleared.

The file must be a file of the selected session's folder, at most 16 MiB (a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; a larger one is refused with not posted: the file is larger than the chat carries), and not one of the chat's own settings files. A picture opened from the chat is not a file of the project, so with one in the preview nothing is posted and the status line says so for 10 seconds: not posted: the preview shows a picture opened from the chat, which is not a file of this project; show a file in the preview, then press Ctrl+u. Select the file in Files mode, click in the Chat panel and press Ctrl+u again. Nothing is posted either while the channel list or the name prompt is open.

Pictures ​

A message's pictures (PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP) show below its text as square tiles, each with Slack's thumbnail once it arrives; any other file shows as a line with its name. A click on a tile (pressed and released in place) opens that picture in the preview pane, and Ctrl+o opens the marked message's first picture. The status line says opening the picture…, then picture shown in the preview, or why it was not opened.

A picture opened from the chat is not a project file: it has no name in Files mode, Ctrl+u does not post it, and the preview's own tools (drawing over a picture for the agent, cropping it, its scale bar) do not work on it. Moving the cursor in Files mode, or selecting another session, shows a file again. A very large picture is shown at reduced resolution.

Selecting and copying ​

Press the left mouse button on a message's text and drag to select within that message; the name and time line is not selected. Ctrl+c (or Ctrl+Shift+c) copies the selection as shown: a link's label, a mention's name, a style's text without its marks. With nothing selected it copies the marked message's text. (edited) is never copied. The status line says copied chat text, or nothing to copy: select text or mark a message. The session's agent never sees what you select in the chat; it sees only text you select in the preview pane and what you draw there (Drawing and selecting for the agent).

How messages look ​

The panel is dark, like Slack's desktop app in dark mode. Each message starts with a header: the author's name in bold, a small gray badge, and the time on a 12-hour clock (2:14 PM). The badge is AGENT for a post from one of your sessions' agents (sent through your Slack app) and APP for any other post from a Slack app; your own posts carry none. Messages from one author less than five minutes apart on the same day share one header, with a small colored square in the margin; mark such a message to see its time in the margin. A divider names each day (Today, Yesterday, Monday, November 9th). A message Slack marks as edited ends with (edited).

Slack's styles show as styles: *bold*, _italic_, ~strike~, , fenced code blocks and > quotes. Links show their label (a link is not clickable), people as @ and their name, channels as # and their name, and @here, @channel and @everyone by name. A name not looked up yet shows as its label or its ID until the channel is read again. Any other markup shows as typed, in blue.

The agent in the channel ​

A session's agent can post to its project's channel with the command sot-fe chat post, which Ship of Tools installs for agents to run. Its posts appear as <handle> (row via Ship of Tools), where the handle is the session's name as Sessions mode shows it, the name it also signs its messages to other sessions with (Agents messaging each other). In the commands below, <ts> is a message's Slack timestamp, which names it.

A reply from the agent ​

The agent runs sot-fe chat post [--thread <ts>] (<text> | --stdin) in the session. The post goes into the thread named by --thread, or else is a new message. It is sent once: the command prints posted <ts> and exits 0; exits 2 with refused and a reason, or limited, wait <n> s, when nothing was posted; and exits 3 with unknown or no answer when the post may exist, in which case the agent does not post it again and reads the thread first. A reply is public to every member of the channel.

A figure from the agent ​

Ask the session's agent to post a figure, and it runs sot-fe chat post [--thread <ts>] --file <path> [<comment> | --stdin] in the session. The file must be in the session's folder: a relative path is taken from the folder the command runs in, and a path outside the folder or with a .. in it is refused before anything is sent. A link inside the folder is followed, so a data/results link to another disk can be posted; the chat's own settings files are refused. Any regular file of at most 16 MiB (a fixed limit of Ship of Tools; a larger one is refused, exit 2) can be posted; Slack shows it under <handle> (row via Ship of Tools), then the comment. With --thread it is posted into that thread; if it shows in the channel instead, Slack did not take the thread. The command prints posted <ts>, or posted file <id> when Slack names no message, with the same exit codes as a reply.

Mail to the session ​

Posts in a project's channel can reach the session's agent as mail (the messages sessions receive from each other), so the agent can answer in the channel with no window open.

The rest of this section is for whoever administers the computer that runs your sessions.

Mail is off until mail_after, the time from which posts count as mail (a Slack timestamp, <seconds>.<six digits>), is set in chat.toml, the chat's settings file; the chat configuration says how. Then each new post a channel member makes in a mapped channel (one picked for a project), yours from the panel included, reaches that project's session as a message to its agent. An idle Claude Code agent starts on it at once, a busy one reads it when it finishes what it is doing, and a Codex agent reads it the next time it checks its messages. An agent's own reply is not mail and does not come back to it. A post missed while the computer was down is filed later, once; the configuration page says how.

Moving the chat to another computer ​

For whoever administers your computers.

The chat runs on the computer named on the hub = line of hosts.toml. To move it, change that line and start Ship of Tools' service (sotd, the background program that runs the sessions) on the new computer; nothing else. The old computer's chat stops within a minute, after filing the one post it is filing, and the new one starts within a minute after that. A post made in the minute around the move can reach its session as mail up to 6 hours late: the new computer finds it only at its next check for missed posts, and those checks are never more than 6 hours apart.
