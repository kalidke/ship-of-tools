# rust/frontend/src/ui/app: the winit application (fe-ui)

The window's application shell: `App` holds the window's `State` and its startup inputs, and the winit callbacks run
the event loop against it. Part of fe-ui; charter: rust/frontend/src/ui/CLAUDE.md. ADR 0050 describes the exit and
hand-over it serves.

## Files
- `mod.rs`: App, its constructor and run/finalizer boundary, `MainWake` (how another thread wakes the window) and
  `MainWindow` (State's window handle, `!Send`, which a compile-time check asserts; in its own module `main_window`,
  so its `Arc<Window>` reaches only wgpu's surface, through `create_surface`), plus FRAME_BUDGET and
  NAV_FIRE_DEBOUNCE.
- `exit_process_tests.rs`: bounded runtime and process-exit cases, run through the shared isolation helper.
- `exit.rs`: quit_prompt_step, request_quit, begin_leave, leave, confirm_stay, stay_open, finish_exit, redraw_exits and the per-App ExitDeadline shared with State.
- `handler.rs`: `impl ApplicationHandler for App`: `resumed`, `window_event`, `about_to_wait`, `user_event`,
  `new_events`.
- `frame.rs`: `State::redraw`, one frame's sequence, its upkeep (`frame_upkeep`) and `ack_presented_lines`.
- `native_children.rs`: the window_minimized target's children and parent: the start its two children share, the State fixtures' own child (the native-only fixtures of the result-routing, badge, account, canonical-row and Monitor commits, one `state-fixture name=... ok=...` line each, all run before the child judges) and the parent, which runs both children and then judges. It also owns HarnessTick, the test-only pending tick counter and dispatch shared by the progress, close and pane drivers.
- `native_eventlog.rs`: Windows only: the Application log's Application Hang events (1002) for one fixture child; an unreadable log is an error.
- `native_exit_tests.rs`: the opt-in main-thread native window-close fixture, using the actual App callbacks and test-owned inputs; the parent runs each close case as a child of its binary.
- `native_harness_tests.rs`: display-free behavioral tests of the native harness helpers, declared by the feature-gated `tests.rs`; native targets still run their own main-thread children.
- `native_pane_daemon_tests.rs`: the pane_timing parent's private daemon: its own roots, rows created Ready or seeded with no supervisor, planted screen sentinels, and one window lease the parent holds for its whole life; teardown is that lease's Close (zero not ended) and the daemon's own exit, and a parent that dies without the Close leaves the daemon to end every row and exit.
- `native_pane_route_tests.rs`: the pane_timing routes: a `sotd stdio-bridge` stand-in, or two real ssh logins through a test-owned stand-in for the hub's relay unit; and the preflight that proves the route reaches the private daemon.
- `native_pane_tests.rs`: the opt-in native relayed-attach timing fixture: the parent runs three timing children and one fault child of its binary; each timing child switches once to every row through the real App and reports each attach's first presentation receipt and the current pane cells at the next harness tick.
- `tests.rs`: the native minimized-window event-progress workload; test-owned inputs, no daemon or user settings. It runs in a child of its own, after the State fixtures' child (`native_children.rs`), and starts its visible phase after the window's first three frames. Its visible phase starts with the window's thread held for `MAIN_HOLD`; under `test-mac-redraw-mutation` its transport wakes the window by a `Window` call instead of `MainWake`, and the workload child ends at the visible phase's verdict (`mac-redraw-mutation visible failures=...`).

## Start here
`redraw` in frame.rs for the order of one frame; `window_event` in handler.rs for which winit event goes where and for
the redraw arm; `keyboard_input` and `route_key` in ui/input/keypress.rs and the pointer functions in ui/input/mouse.rs
for any input change; `about_to_wait` for wake-up scheduling and the `[display] fullscreen_vsync_pin` setting;
`request_quit` in exit.rs for how the window closes.

## Rules
- Every user quit goes through `request_quit`: `exit_intent` asks on Ctrl+Q and leaves at once on the close button.
- begin_leave can access only lease and exit-state slots and returns Redraw or Finish; leave_close_keep_and_handover_only_leave_leases drives that production transition and observes pending exit and actual lease frames. No agent or drawer input capability is passed to it.
- A leaving window exits from `about_to_wait`'s poll once the acks are in (`redraw_exits`), except that a Keep or a Handover still waiting on an unanswered lease request at its deadline, or at a Ctrl+Q during the wait, stays open: confirm_stay, over begin_leave's exit slots and the status line, ends the leave, clears `should_exit` and clears the status (a relaunch command's "relaunching…" would contradict the lease notice) once `Leases::stay` confirms it, and a_stay_keeps_the_window_open_until_the_next_leave drives it.
- A harness instance (`ephemeral`) starts neither watcher thread: `resumed` skips `relaunch::spawn_watcher` and `spawn_command_watcher` for it.
- Frames are capped at `FRAME_BUDGET` (`window_event`'s redraw arm and `about_to_wait`).
- The idle repaint (the 1 s clock, the 80 ms flash and 16 ms help fades) keeps its armed deadline across wakes (`arm_idle`, which owns the slot): a wake brings it forward or leaves it, never later.
- A pending `MainWake` becomes the window's own redraw in `user_event`; at most one wake event is outstanding.
  A native driver forwards every user event to App before consuming its own ticker's pending count. A positive consumption advances its harness once; progress sample credit increases by the consumed count, while close and pane perform one step. An unmarked wake adds no work. The ticker requests no redraw. Pane window-event and wait callbacks call App directly without advancing the harness.
- A frame runs in `redraw`'s fixed order: upkeep, the chrome draw, the pixel layout, the text prepare, one render pass,
  then the capture's staging, submit, present, the acks and the capture's write.
- While the quit prompt is open, non-repeat Tab toggles and Enter confirms by logical key identity, every other non-repeat key cancels, and repeats do nothing; input routing consumes the event before modifier-only suppression and later dispatch.
- A second close, unless it is a Ctrl+Q that stays (confirm_stay), resolves close_now's code and makes one bounded deliver_queued attempt before the window finishes; a write timeout is reported and is not a daemon acknowledgement.
- Every return from run_app, including an error or capture completion, takes the transport runtime once and calls shutdown_timeout(LEAVE_WRITE_WAIT) before App drops. Timed-out blocking work may continue; the yielding-child cleanup proof does not promise cancellation of arbitrary synchronous work.
- The final window decision arms one three-second std-thread process backstop before a forced queued-write attempt or final event-loop exit; capture and returned-loop failure also enter this finalization. Prompt time and daemon acknowledgement/presentation time precede that deadline.
- Codes 0, 75 and 76 are preserved, including a Close superseding Handover; the historical immediate nonzero branch retains its foreground handover.
- Process-exit tests stall between the arm under test and every later arming opportunity: forced delivery stalls before finish_exit, a loop return stalls before fallback arming, nonzero finish_exit stalls before its direct exit, and fallback cases stall after fallback arming. Reversals remove only the production arm and must fail their own exit assertion.
- Ordinary native close must exit 0 before 2.5 seconds without the backstop; deliberate stalled teardown must end under the three-second backstop with the decided code. The native cases are OS close, Ctrl+Q then No, a second close during a held Close, a Close whose acknowledgement is held past three seconds and whose not-ended notice is presented, relaunch 75 and 76, capture through a real State, and closes held at the loop return, before delivery and before the direct exit. On Windows each ordinary case also requires no Application Hang event correlated to the fixture's image and process id.
- Native progress evidence separates producer workload validity from UI queue progress; counters are taken at successful fan-in enqueue and actual State dequeue, and the fixture never drains the queue.
- The State fixtures rewire State (its event receiver among them), so they never run in the workload's process.
- A frame consumes its local pane presentation candidate only after submit and present; an earlier frame error drops the candidate without completing the attach.
- A pane timing sample uses the first presentation receipt's own `since_request_ns` for a cold attach (its row never the pane target earlier in its child). The checkpoint must precede that receipt. At the next harness tick the current pane cells must hold the row's planted sentinel; the driver reports that tick's elapsed time, which may exceed the receipt time by at most 50 ms. These cells are not evidence of the exact receipt frame. The sample also reports the open, checkpoint and attached phases. Every Ready sample must be within `CONNECT_BOUND`; resume samples are recorded apart and not held to it. Only a missing display or adapter is "not runnable"; any other setup or child failure fails. A child that is not ready to switch within 90 s fails naming its state: whether its rows were listed, every host key's list and its length, its frame count, its pane size, its active host and status line, and its window thread's last failed redraw.
- Under `test-pane-timing` only, `redraw` can fail the first frame that carries a presentation candidate before submit (the pane_timing fault child); that frame completes nothing and the next one completes the attach. Ordinary builds have no such hook.
- The close and pane parents wait on each child through `sot_log::test_isolated`'s drained `wait_and_read`, which keeps both streams when the child overruns; the window_minimized children stream their output into the parent's, which waits on them with the plain `wait_until`; each parent runs all its cases or children and then judges, except that the close and pane parents stop at a case or child that is "not runnable here"; a pane failure prints the fault child's verdict line first; a close child that has not drawn three frames in 20 s fails, saying how many it drew.
- A pane_timing child ends when its parent's pipe to its stdin closes; the parent awaits each child and the daemon without panicking, and the kill-proof run kills a mid-run parent and requires every process it started to end and every row to be ended by the daemon.
- Every pane_timing child uses its own in-memory Laptop preset, reports the primary monitor's size and aspect and its resolved columns before switching, and includes those fields in a setup failure. The parent publishes each child's layout receipt on success too. Product layout selection is unchanged.
- Native progress bookkeeping performs no output under its ledger lock. Actual accepted times update per-second bins and maximum lateness; actual dequeues update sequence identity and final-drain time. A report owns a scalar snapshot and writes after unlocking. Computing its ten-second rates visits at most twice the scheduled phase's seconds, independent of event count. The workload and queue predicates keep their existing bounds. The reported samples count is consumed ticker credit within the phase; each consumed batch checks the actual native state once. Every progress snapshot prints samples, including body entry and completion.
- The close fixture copies its fake-daemon log before printing; pane output uses an owned capture snapshot. A harness prints with none of its bookkeeping locks held.
